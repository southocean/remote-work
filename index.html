Where to be

Distraction comes from your routine more than from the city. A quiet address with a gym in the building, 15 minutes by metro from District 1, gets you most of Saigon without the nightly temptations. A smaller city gets you quiet for free, but you give up the fun you'd actually enjoy.

Proposed: spend the first month in Saigon along Metro Line 1 (Thảo Điền / An Phú, or a high-rise near Văn Thánh / Tân Cảng). Live there over Tết, then review. If the city keeps winning over the writing, move to Đà Lạt or Đà Nẵng for a 6–8 week writing block.

Metro Line 1: what it covers and costs
  • Runs Bến Thành → Suối Tiên (~20 km, 14 stations), heading east through Bình Thạnh, Thảo Điền, An Phú and Thủ Đức. It's the only line so far; more are under construction.
  • Single ride is roughly 7k–20k ₫ depending on distance. The monthly pass is about 300k ₫ (~110 SEK). Check current fares when you arrive.
  • Living near a station on the eastern stretch means ~10–15 min to Bến Thành with no Grab needed. Combine it with Grab or Xanh SM (electric taxis) for everything off the line.
  • Line 1 doesn't reach D3, D7 or Phú Nhuận, so if you choose those, Grab is your metro.

The apartment in Sweden

Check this first: is it a hyresrätt (rental) or a bostadsrätt (owned)? A hyresrätt needs the landlord's written approval to sublet, and "working abroad for a limited time" is an accepted reason. A bostadsrätt needs approval from the BRF board. Either way, approval is usually granted for up to a year at a time.
  • Confirm contract type and who approves the sublet
  • Send the written sublet request (dates, reason, tenant once known)
  • Set rent within the rules. Hyresrätt: your rent plus a modest furnished surcharge (roughly 10–15%). Bostadsrätt: your actual costs, under the cost-based rules
  • List it. Qasa handles the contract, payments and deposit; Blocket or friends-of-friends also work
  • Written contract with fixed dates, deposit, inventory list, and who pays electricity and internet
  • Tell your home insurer about the sublet. The tenant gets their own hemförsäkring
  • Note the rental income for next year's tax return (there's a 40k SEK/year standard deduction for private letting)

Pack-out

  • Eat-down plan: stop big grocery runs ~3 weeks before. Freezer first, then pantry, then fridge
  • Defrost the freezer and leave the fridge clean (or stocked with basics if the tenant moves in right away)
  • Costumes: vacuum bags into the förråd, or store with a friend. Free up at least one closet for the tenant
  • Lock away or take valuables, documents and anything sentimental
  • Deep clean, or hire a cleaner for a flyttstäd-level clean
  • Photograph every room and write down meter readings on handover day
  • Mail: PostNord eftersändning to a friend, plus Kivra for digital mail
  • Pause or keep subscriptions: gym card, streaming, broadband (keep it if it's included in the rent)
  • Spare key with a trusted friend as the local contact for the tenant
  • Plants to a friend

Work

Time zones work in your favour. Vietnam is UTC+7, which is 6 hours ahead of Sweden in winter (5 hours from 28 Mar). Swedish 09:00–17:00 is 15:00–23:00 in Vietnam, so every morning is free for writing.
  • Write a one-page proposal: dates, overlap hours (e.g. 15:00–21:00 VN = 09:00–15:00 SE), how to reach you, a trial period
  • Meet your manager, then get the approval in writing
  • Ask HR: are they OK with you working from abroad for 5 months (insurance, data and security policy, tax)?
  • VPN and company access tested from the new laptop before you leave
  • 2FA: authenticator app plus backup codes. Don't rely only on SMS to a Swedish number
  • Recurring meetings moved into the overlap window
  • Kit: X13 G2, charger plus a spare USB-C PD charger, headset, maybe a portable monitor and laptop stand
  • Backups: everything in git or cloud storage, writing synced (Koofr)
  • Test week: work a full week on the laptop only, in Sweden, before you fly

Admin & paperwork

Entry & staying

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months past your return date
  • Visa route. A Vietnamese passport needs nothing. On a Swedish passport, the visa-free stay is short (45 days), so get a 90-day e-visa, or better, the 5-year visa exemption certificate for overseas Vietnamese (up to 180 days per entry)
  • Make sure the landlord registers your stay (đăng ký tạm trú). It's required, and they usually handle it

Tax & registration

  • Stay under 183 days in Vietnam in any 12-month period, so you don't become a Vietnamese tax resident
  • You stay registered in Sweden (you only deregister for moves over 1 year). No Skatteverket action needed, but confirm with HR

Health & insurance

  • Swedish healthcare doesn't cover you outside the EU, and the travel cover in your home insurance usually ends after 45–60 days. Buy long-stay travel insurance for the full period
  • Vaccines: Hep A/B, typhoid, tetanus booster (ask a travel clinic). Pack mosquito repellent for dengue
  • Dentist and any prescriptions sorted before you leave

Money & phone

  • Tell your bank you're travelling. Get a Wise or Revolut card for cheap VND
  • Vietnam runs on QR payments (VietQR). A local bank app makes daily life much easier
  • Keep the Swedish number on a cheap plan or eSIM for SMS codes. BankID works abroad over wifi
  • Vietnamese SIM registered with your passport (Viettel), plus a backup SIM from Mobifone or Vinaphone
  • Plugs: Vietnam is 220V with A/C sockets. Swedish chargers fit with a cheap adapter

Daily life

🍗 Eating for muscle

Aim for roughly 1.6–2 g of protein per kg per day. Vietnamese food is rice-heavy but easy to tilt toward protein:

  • Cơm gà / cơm tấm: ask for extra meat (thêm thịt) and an egg, and go easy on the rice
  • Phở bò with extra beef, bò lúc lắc, bún chả, grilled fish, đậu hũ
  • Eggs and Greek yoghurt from WinMart / Co.op / Bách Hóa Xanh
  • Healthy meal-prep subscriptions (~70–120k ₫/meal) delivered weekly
  • Whey is easy to find (Wheyshop, Thol). Bring your shaker
  • Watch the sugar: cà phê sữa, sinh tố and chè add up fast

🏋️ Training

  • Easiest: pick an apartment with a gym in the building (common in Vinhomes / Masteri-type high-rises)
  • Saigon chains: CitiGym, California Fitness, Elite. Roughly 800k–1.5M ₫/month
  • Local iron gyms: 300–500k ₫/month, often excellent and no-frills
  • Train early (06–08) or in the evening, since midday heat is brutal
  • Đà Nẵng has plenty of gyms. Đà Lạt has fewer, so check before you commit

🚇 Getting around

  • Saigon: Metro Line 1 pass (~300k ₫), plus Grab, Xanh SM or Be for the rest
  • Grab bike for short trips (~15–30k ₫), Grab car when it's raining
  • Renting a motorbike needs a Vietnamese licence or an IDP (1968 convention). Without one, your insurance won't pay
  • Đà Lạt / Đà Nẵng have no metro, so expect Grab or a motorbike

📶 Internet

  • A 5G/4G pocket router plus a data SIM (Viettel) gives you wifi anywhere, around 200–300k ₫/month
  • Most rentals also include fibre, so test the speed at the viewing
  • Undersea cable cuts occasionally slow international traffic, so keep a second carrier's SIM as backup
  • Cafés and coworking spaces (Saigon has dozens) are your fallback office

Guardrails against Saigon
  • Weeknights: home by 23:30. Mornings are not negotiable.
  • Friends, nightlife and exploring go on Friday night and Saturday. Sunday is for planning and a light write.
  • Keep a weekly word count or pages log, and review it every Sunday. If two weeks in a row slip, move to the quiet city.
  • Tell friends and family the schedule up front, so "I'm writing" is understood as a real no.
